\section*{1 $\cdot$ Motivation}

\textbf{Start on-policy.} The vanilla policy-gradient (REINFORCE) objective is


\begin{equation*}
\nabla J(\theta) \;=\; \mathbb E_{s\sim d^{\pi_\theta},\,a\sim \pi_\theta}\!\left[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)\cdot \hat A(s,a)\right].
\end{equation*}


Both the state distribution $d^{\pi_\theta}$ and the action distribution $\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s)$ are under the \emph{current} policy --- the algorithm is statistically correct only when every rollout used to compute the gradient came from today's $\pi_\theta$. For LLMs this is prohibitive: rollouts cost far more than gradient steps, so we want to \textbf{reuse samples across updates} (multi-epoch PPO), collect them asynchronously (async RL frameworks), or continue using a rollout even after the policy has drifted.

\textbf{Going off-policy forces an importance-sampling correction.} If rollouts come from a behaviour policy $\pi_\beta\neq\pi_\theta$, the on-policy expectation can be rewritten, exactly, as


\begin{equation*}
\nabla J(\theta) \;=\; \mathbb E_{s\sim d^{\pi_\beta},\,a\sim \pi_\beta}\!\left[\underbrace{\tfrac{d^{\pi_\theta}(s)}{d^{\pi_\beta}(s)}}_{\text{state ratio}}\cdot \underbrace{\tfrac{\pi_\theta(a\mid s)}{\pi_\beta(a\mid s)}}_{\text{action ratio}}\cdot \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a\mid s)\cdot \hat A(s,a)\right].
\end{equation*}


\textbf{Two IS correction terms} appear --- one for the state distribution, one for the action. Both are needed for the identity to hold.

\textbf{The TRPO/PPO/GRPO lineage: keep one, drop one.} The state ratio is notoriously hard to estimate in a general MDP (the numerator requires knowing $d^{\pi_\theta}$, which has no closed form), so a long line of methods takes the same two-step shortcut:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Drop the state ratio} --- set $d^{\pi_\theta}(s)/d^{\pi_\beta}(s)\equiv 1$.
\item \textbf{Compensate by constraining the action ratio} close to $1$ --- via a KL trust region (TRPO), a clipped ratio (PPO/GRPO/DAPO), a geometric-mean sequence ratio (GSPO), a soft gate (SAPO), or a direct divergence (DPPO).
\end{enumerate}

The justification is always the same: \textbf{if $\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s)$ stays close to $\pi_\beta(\cdot\mid s)$ at every state, then $d^{\pi_\theta}\approx d^{\pi_\beta}$} (a standard TRPO-style performance-difference bound), so the state-ratio-free surrogate is a good approximation of the true off-policy gradient. Every GRPO variant can be read as a different answer to "\emph{how} do we keep the action ratio in check?" --- but none touches the state ratio directly. This is the entire design space of the last decade of policy-gradient methods for language models: one dropped term, many action-side trust-region formulations.

The approximation is tight in the strict on-policy limit. It degrades under moderate off-policy drift --- async rollouts, multi-epoch updates, long sequences --- precisely the regimes modern LLM training is trending toward. And it is, structurally, a workaround: restricting the action ratio to avoid a state-ratio problem you'd rather not compute.

\textbf{Key observation.} For LLMs the state ratio is not intractable. Transitions are deterministic: $s_{t+1}=(q,o_{\le t})$ is uniquely determined by $(s_t,a_t)$, so


\begin{equation*}
d^{\pi}(s_t) \;=\; \prod_{j=1}^{t-1}\pi(o_j\mid q, o_{<j})
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\boxed{\;\tfrac{d^{\pi_\theta}(s_t)}{d^{\pi_\beta}(s_t)} \;=\; \prod_{j=1}^{t-1} r_j(\theta),\qquad r_j(\theta)=\tfrac{\pi_\theta(o_j\mid q, o_{<j})}{\pi_\beta(o_j\mid q, o_{<j})}.\;}
\end{equation*}


This identity is \textbf{exact} --- every $r_j$ is already computed during training. The question shifts from "how hard should we clamp the action ratio to fake $d^{\pi_\theta}\approx d^{\pi_\beta}$?" to "\textbf{how do we directly use $\prod_{j<t} r_j$ without the classical exponential-variance explosion of trajectory IS?}" The rest of this paper answers the second question.

\section*{2 $\cdot$ The SC-GRPO Objective}

Let $r_t$ be the per-token action ratio and $w_t$ an \textbf{exclusive state weight} depending only on the prefix $o_{<t}$ (various surrogates in §3):


\begin{equation*}
\boxed{\;L_{\text{SC}}(\theta) \;=\; -\,\mathbb{E}_{o\sim \pi_\beta}\!\left[\sum_t \; w_t\cdot r_t\cdot \log\pi_\theta(o_t\mid q, o_{<t})\cdot \hat A_t\cdot m_t\right].\;}
\end{equation*}


Both $w_t$ and $r_t$ are detached; gradient flows only through $\log\pi_\theta$. The unified trust-region gate is


\begin{equation*}
m_t \;=\; \mathbf 1[\,w_t\in [w_{\min}, w_{\max}]\,]\cdot \mathbf 1[\,r_t\in[1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]\,].
\end{equation*}


SC clamp and PPO clip are treated symmetrically as multiplicative gradient masks --- we call this \textbf{Plan A}. It produces a single diagnostic (\texttt{joint\_active\_frac}) that faithfully reflects which tokens drive the gradient.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Implementation note.} For state correction to be meaningful, \texttt{old\_log\_prob} must be the true rollout log-prob $\log\pi_\beta$, not a recomputed $\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$. We obtain it via \texttt{verl}'s \texttt{bypass\_mode}, which feeds vllm-side rollout log-probs through as old log-probs --- saving one actor forward pass and guaranteeing $\pi_\beta$ is exact.

\end{quote}
\vspace{0.5em}\hrule\vspace{0.5em}
\section*{3 $\cdot$ A Unified View of $w_{i,t}$}

Strip every PPO-family method down to its policy-gradient shape. Ignoring trust-region gates (clip / clamp --- they are gradient masks, not part of the off-policy IS structure), every method below can be written as a per-token objective of the form


\begin{equation*}
L(\theta) \;=\; -\,\mathbb E_{\{o_i\}\sim\pi_\beta}\!\left[\sum_i\sum_{t=1}^{|o_i|}\; w_{i,t}\cdot r_{i,t}\cdot \hat A_{i,t}\cdot \log\pi_\theta(o_{i,t}\mid q, o_{i,<t})\right],
\end{equation*}


which gives the gradient


\begin{equation*}
\nabla L(\theta) \;=\; -\,\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{i,t}\; w_{i,t}\cdot r_{i,t}\cdot \hat A_{i,t}\cdot \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(o_{i,t})\right],
\end{equation*}


where $r_{i,t}=\pi_\theta(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})/\pi_\beta(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t})$ is the \textbf{per-token action ratio} and $w_{i,t}$ is the \textbf{state weight}. The exact off-policy objective has $w_{i,t}^{\text{exact}}=\rho_{i,1:t-1}=\prod_{j<t}r_{i,j}$. Every method differs from the exact case in exactly one place: what it puts in $w_{i,t}$.

\subsection*{3.1 GRPO variants, reduced to a common form}

\textbf{GRPO.} The published objective (ignoring clip):


\begin{equation*}
\mathcal J_{\text{GRPO}} \;=\; \mathbb E\!\left[\tfrac{1}{G}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\frac{1}{|o_i|}\sum_{t=1}^{T} r_{i,t}\,\hat A_{i,t}\right].
\end{equation*}


The $\frac{1}{G}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\frac{1}{|o_i|}\sum_{t=1}^{T}$ factor is a Monte-Carlo aggregation choice (estimator of the outer expectation), not a per-token weight specified by the algorithm. Dropping it into the outer expectation, the per-token shape is $r_{i,t}\hat A_{i,t}\cdot\log\pi_\theta$ --- i.e., $w_{i,t}=1$.


\begin{equation*}
\boxed{\;w_{i,t}^{\text{GRPO}} \;=\; 1.\;}
\end{equation*}


\textbf{DAPO.} Same as GRPO except the aggregation is changed to \texttt{token-mean} (a correct MC estimator) and the clip becomes asymmetric. Neither changes $w_{i,t}$:


\begin{equation*}
\mathcal J_{\text{DAPO}} \;=\; \mathbb E\!\left[\tfrac{1}{\sum_{i'}|o_{i'}|}\sum_{i,t} r_{i,t}\,\hat A_{i,t}\right]
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\boxed{\;w_{i,t}^{\text{DAPO}}=1.\;}
\end{equation*}


\textbf{GSPO.} Defines a sequence-level ratio $s_i=\big(\prod_{j=1}^{|o_i|}r_{i,j}\big)^{1/|o_i|}$ and writes


\begin{equation*}
\mathcal J_{\text{GSPO}} \;=\; \mathbb E\!\left[\tfrac{1}{G}\sum_i s_i\,\hat A_i\right].
\end{equation*}


The gradient is $\nabla s_i = s_i\cdot\tfrac{1}{|o_i|}\sum_t\nabla\log\pi_\theta(o_{i,t})$. Apply the identity $\nabla\log\pi_\theta = \tfrac{1}{r_{i,t}}\cdot r_{i,t}\nabla\log\pi_\theta$ once to align with the common form:


\begin{equation*}
\nabla\mathcal J_{\text{GSPO}} \;=\; \mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{i,t}\;\tfrac{s_i}{|o_i|\,r_{i,t}}\cdot r_{i,t}\nabla\log\pi_\theta\cdot\hat A_i\right]
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\boxed{\;w_{i,t}^{\text{GSPO}} \;=\; \tfrac{s_i}{|o_i|\,r_{i,t}}.\;}
\end{equation*}


$s_i$ depends on the whole response including future tokens $o_{>t}$, so $w_{i,t}^{\text{GSPO}}$ is not state-measurable: each per-token weight is a function of the entire trajectory rather than just its prefix, which is a different design choice from the prefix-only $w_{i,t}^{\text{SC}}$ we study below.

\subsection*{3.2 The unified comparison}

Our SC family replaces $w_{i,t}=1$ by a prefix-based surrogate that targets the exact $\rho_{i,1:t-1}$. Three concrete choices span the natural design space: the trivial ablation $w_{i,t}=1$ (recovers GRPO), the exact prefix product $\rho_{i,1:t-1}$ (our main method), and a conservative prefix-min surrogate $\min_{j<t}r_{i,j}$ (a lower-bound substitute for $\rho_{i,1:t-1}$, included as an ablation). Each non-trivial variant is wrapped by a symmetric outer clamp $[w_{\min},w_{\max}]=[1/c, c]$ with $c\!\in\!\{2,4\}$ as a safety net on accumulated prefix drift. We will see in §3.4 that this clamp is largely redundant once the standard PPO token-level clip is in place, and that the specific value of $c$ affects neither final performance nor PPO clip activation. To position these against the published methods of §3.1, the exact weight $\rho_{i,1:t-1}$ satisfies
\[
\mathbb E_{\pi_\beta}[\log\rho_{1:t-1}] \approx -(t-1)\bar D_{\mathrm{KL}},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_\beta}[\log\rho_{1:t-1}] \lesssim (t-1)\sigma^2,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_\beta}[\rho_{1:t-1}] \gtrsim e^{(t-1)\sigma^2}-1,
\]
i.e.\ the log-weight drifts linearly negative (Jensen) while the weight itself has nominally exponential variance --- the latter being precisely the term the per-token PPO clip already gates (§3.4). Putting all methods into the common form of §3.1:

Reading the table:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{GRPO, DAPO, \texttt{SC-identity}}: no prefix signal --- the state correction is absent.
\item \textbf{GSPO}: uses all tokens, but non-causally (each $w_{i,t}$ depends on the entire response through $s_i$).
\item \textbf{\texttt{SC-min-prefix}}: causal prefix, but a conservative lower-bound surrogate (always $\le \rho_{i,1:t-1}$); biased. Included as an ablation against the exact prefix product.
\item \textbf{SC-mask $\star$}: the only row that uses the \emph{exact} prefix-product weight $\rho_{i,1:t-1}$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:is-unbiased} below, $\rho_{1:t-1}\!\cdot\! r_t$ is a strictly unbiased per-token IS estimator of the on-policy gradient. The classical concern with such estimators is exponential variance --- which, in our setting, is addressed not by a numerical clamp on $w$ but by a per-token mask that zeros out both the magnitude and the gradient of pathological tokens (§3.4). The mask threshold $c$ controls \emph{which} tokens are flagged; final accuracy is essentially insensitive to its specific value (§5.4).
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{3.3 Theoretical guarantees}

We now formalise the property of \texttt{SC-mask} that justifies its design. Throughout, we assume the LLM rollout MDP with deterministic transitions described in §1, and write $\rho_{i,1:t-1}=\prod_{j<t}r_{i,j}$ for the prefix product of per-token action ratios. All expectations are over $o\sim\pi_\beta$ unless stated otherwise.

\begin{theorem}[Per-token IS factorisation: $w_t\!\cdot\! r_t$ is unbiased]
\label{thm:is-unbiased}
For every fixed step $t$ and any state-measurable advantage estimate $\hat A_t$,
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_{o\sim\pi_\beta}\!\left[\,\rho_{1:t-1}\cdot r_t\cdot \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(o_t\mid q,o_{<t})\cdot \hat A_t\,\right]
\;=\;
\mathbb E_{o\sim\pi_\theta}\!\left[\,\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(o_t\mid q,o_{<t})\cdot \hat A_t\,\right].
\end{equation*}
That is, the per-token product $w_t^{\mathrm{exact}}\cdot r_t$, with $w_t^{\mathrm{exact}}=\rho_{1:t-1}$, is an \textbf{exact (unbiased) importance-sampling estimator} of the on-policy per-token policy gradient.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The joint behaviour density on $o_{\le t}$ factorises as $\pi_\beta(o_{\le t}\mid q)=\prod_{j=1}^{t}\pi_\beta(o_j\mid q,o_{<j})$, and similarly for $\pi_\theta$. Since transitions are deterministic, the state $s_t=(q,o_{<t})$ is a function of the prefix $o_{<t}$ alone, hence
\begin{equation*}
\frac{\pi_\theta(o_{\le t}\mid q)}{\pi_\beta(o_{\le t}\mid q)}
\;=\;\prod_{j=1}^{t-1}\frac{\pi_\theta(o_j\mid q,o_{<j})}{\pi_\beta(o_j\mid q,o_{<j})}\cdot\frac{\pi_\theta(o_t\mid q,o_{<t})}{\pi_\beta(o_t\mid q,o_{<t})}
\;=\;\rho_{1:t-1}\cdot r_t .
\end{equation*}
A standard change of measure on the joint distribution of $o_{\le t}$ then gives
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E_{\pi_\theta}\!\left[g(o_{\le t})\right]
\;=\;\mathbb E_{\pi_\beta}\!\left[\frac{\pi_\theta(o_{\le t}\mid q)}{\pi_\beta(o_{\le t}\mid q)}\,g(o_{\le t})\right]
\;=\;\mathbb E_{\pi_\beta}\!\left[\rho_{1:t-1}\cdot r_t\cdot g(o_{\le t})\right]
\end{equation*}
for any integrable $g$. Taking $g=\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(o_t\mid s_t)\cdot \hat A_t$ (which depends on $o_{\le t}$ through $s_t=(q,o_{<t})$ and the sampled action $o_t$) yields the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The two factors play distinct roles: the \emph{state weight} $\rho_{1:t-1}$ is the marginal density correction $d^{\pi_\theta}(s_t)/d^{\pi_\beta}(s_t)$ (§1), while the \emph{action weight} $r_t$ is the conditional correction $\pi_\theta(o_t\mid s_t)/\pi_\beta(o_t\mid s_t)$. Theorem~\ref{thm:is-unbiased} is the statement that the standard PPO/GRPO objective, which keeps only $r_t$ and silently sets $\rho_{1:t-1}\equiv 1$, is the special case in which the state-distribution correction has been dropped.
\end{remark}

\vspace{0.5em}\hrule\vspace{0.5em}
\subsection*{3.4 Why a numerical clamp is not enough: \texttt{SC-mask}}

Theorem~\ref{thm:is-unbiased} licenses $w_{i,t}=\rho_{i,1:t-1}$ as the unique unbiased state-correction. The textbook objection is that $\rho_{1:t-1}=\prod_{j<t}r_{i,j}$ has variance growing exponentially in $t$. The natural reflex is to bound the per-token contribution numerically:
\[
\text{(``naïve clamp'')}\qquad
\tilde w_{i,t}\!\cdot\!\tilde r_{i,t} \;=\; \mathrm{clip}\!\big(w_{i,t}\!\cdot\! r_{i,t},\;1/c,\;c\big),
\]
plug it into the per-token loss, and rely on the clipped magnitude to keep training stable. \emph{This does not work.} The failure mode is specific to LLM RL and worth spelling out explicitly, because it dictates the design of the actual method we use.

\textbf{What goes wrong with a numerical clamp alone.} An LLM rollout under $\pi_\beta$ inevitably contains a small fraction of \emph{pathological tokens} --- typos, low-mass rare tokens that vllm sampled by chance, end-of-sequence artifacts, formatting noise. For these tokens $\pi_\beta(o_t\mid s_t)$ is tiny, so $r_t=\pi_\theta/\pi_\beta$ is huge before any clamp; the prefix product $\rho_{1:t-1}\!\cdot\!r_t$ explodes. A numerical clamp to $[1/c,c]$ does bound the per-token \emph{magnitude} of the gradient weight --- but the $\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(o_t\mid s_t)$ factor is still computed, and it points in the direction of \emph{raising the probability of this pathological token under $\pi_\theta$}. After the optimizer step, the typo's probability is higher; on the next rollout that direction is reinforced again; within a handful of updates $\pi_\theta$ has shifted measurable mass onto pathological tokens, the rollouts degrade, and training collapses. We observed this collapse consistently in preliminary runs across $c\in\{2,4,8\}$ and across learning-rate scales: pg\_loss diverged within $\sim$10 steps, response length blew up, and the actor began emitting garbled output. \textbf{A bounded-but-nonzero gradient repeatedly applied to a pathological token is still a fatal positive-feedback loop.}

The structural issue is that the standard PPO interpretation of clipping --- ``a slightly-biased gradient is acceptable'' --- relies on the bounded-rewards / stochastic-transitions / small-vocabulary assumption of classical RL. In LLM RL with a $\sim$100k softmax and deterministic transitions, a single pathological token's gradient can lift its probability by several orders of magnitude in one step. The classical exponential-IS-variance worry is real, but it is \emph{not} the failure mode in our setting; the failure mode is targeted gradient corruption on a measure-zero set of tokens that the rollout-vs-train distribution disagrees on.

\textbf{The fix: mask, don't clamp.} What we actually want for tokens whose $w\!\cdot\! r$ would fall outside the trust band is \textbf{no gradient at all}, not a clamped one. Concretely, for token $(i,t)$ we form a Boolean gate
\[
m_{i,t} \;=\; \mathbf 1[\,r_{i,t}\in[1-\epsilon,\,1+\epsilon]\,]\;\cdot\;\mathbf 1[\,w_{i,t}\in[1/c,\,c]\,],
\]
and the per-token contribution to the loss is
\[
\big(\,m_{i,t}\big)\;\cdot\;w_{i,t}\;\cdot\;r_{i,t}\;\cdot\;\hat A_{i,t}\;\cdot\;\log\pi_\theta(o_{i,t}\mid q,o_{i,<t}),
\]
i.e.\ when $m_{i,t}=0$ the entire term vanishes, including the $\nabla\log\pi_\theta$ factor --- the gradient on this token is killed, not scaled. The first $\mathbf 1[\cdot]$ is the standard PPO clip, applied here as a hard mask rather than a min-of-clipped-and-unclipped surrogate; the second is the SC clamp on the prefix-product weight, again as a mask. We call the resulting method \textbf{SC-mask}.

\begin{lstlisting}
log r_j  →  exclusive cumsum  →  w_t = exp(...)  →  build mask m_t  →
m_t * w_t * r_t * A_t * log_pi_theta(o_t)   contributes to loss
# m_t = 0  =>  this token contributes zero loss AND zero gradient
\end{lstlisting}

\textbf{Why $c$ is then nearly insensitive: a semantic-robustness analogy.} Once the gate is in place, the role of $c$ is no longer ``how aggressively to numerically truncate weights'' but ``which tokens to flag as pathological and discard''. Training quality then depends on whether the pool of \emph{kept} tokens carries enough signal to update the policy --- not on the magnitudes of the rejected ones.

This connects to a familiar property of natural language: a sentence with a few typos still conveys its meaning, because semantic content is carried redundantly across many tokens. The same redundancy applies to the policy update: as long as most tokens in a rollout pass the gate and contribute their ($w\!\cdot\! r$)-weighted gradient, the few discarded pathological tokens neither help nor hurt --- they simply do not participate. Tightening $c$ from $4$ to $2$ relabels a few percent of borderline tokens as ``flagged'' rather than ``kept'', but those tokens were near the trust-region boundary anyway, so their contribution to a healthy update was small to begin with. We conjecture this is why empirically the training trajectory is so weakly sensitive to $c$ (§5.4): the per-token \emph{signal} carried by the kept set is robust to small changes in the keep/discard threshold, much as sentence meaning is robust to a small fraction of corrupted tokens.

The practical consequence is that \texttt{SC-mask} introduces \emph{no} new tuning burden over stock GRPO. The only numerical knob remains the PPO clip $\epsilon$ that GRPO already exposes; the SC clamp $c$ is set once (we use $c=2$) and is essentially fixed across runs.

\subsection*{5.4 Off-policy drift, decomposed: $\chi^2_{\mathrm{seq}}$ vs $\chi^2_{\mathrm{token}}$}

The clip / clamp diagnostics in §5.3 measure whether the trust region \emph{fired}, but they do not say \emph{how off-policy} the rollout actually became. \texttt{verl}'s \texttt{rollout\_corr} module logs four numerical proxies for $D(\pi_\beta\|\pi_\theta)$ between the vllm-side rollout log-probs and the actor-side recompute: a per-token KL, a per-token $\chi^2_{\mathrm{token}}\!\approx\!\mathbb E_t[(\log r_t)^2]$, a sequence-level $\chi^2_{\mathrm{seq}}\!\approx\!\mathbb E[(\sum_t \log r_t)^2]$, and the ratio of training-side to rollout-side perplexity. Reading them \emph{jointly} is more informative than any one in isolation.

The two $\chi^2$ metrics measure different things, and they can disagree spectacularly:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{$\chi^2_{\mathrm{seq}}$ is sum-then-square}: $(\sum_t \log r_t)^2$ allows opposite-sign per-token deviations to \emph{cancel}. A trajectory whose log-ratios are $+\Delta,-\Delta,+\Delta,\dots$ scores near zero at sequence level even though every step is far from on-policy.
\item \textbf{$\chi^2_{\mathrm{token}}$ is square-then-sum}: $\mathbb E_t[(\log r_t)^2]$ never lets cancellations happen --- every per-step deviation contributes positively.
\end{itemize}

This decomposition explains the otherwise-paradoxical row \texttt{SC-min-prefix}: $\chi^2_{\mathrm{seq}}\!=\!40$ (\emph{lower} than every other variant in the table), yet $\chi^2_{\mathrm{token}}\!=\!4\,\mathrm{k}$ and KL $\!=\!0.6$ (orders of magnitude higher than \texttt{SC-mask}). Two mechanisms produce this signature simultaneously:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Mode collapse compresses sequence-level mass.} Once \texttt{SC-min-prefix} starts reward-hacking (its weak $\min_{j<t} r_j$ surrogate fails to damp the entropy-runaway loop, §6.3), $\pi_\theta$ concentrates probability onto a few high-reward trajectories. On those few trajectories \emph{both} $\pi_\beta$ and $\pi_\theta$ assign large mass, so $\sum_t\log r_t$ stays small --- $\chi^2_{\mathrm{seq}}$ looks deceptively healthy.
\item \textbf{Per-token mass is reshuffled.} The same collapse spikes individual token probabilities, so single-step $|\log r_t|$ is huge --- $\chi^2_{\mathrm{token}}$ explodes.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{The diagnostic rule.} A run is genuinely on-distribution only when \emph{both} indicators are small. Reading the table top-to-bottom:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \texttt{SC-mask} sits in the safe regime: tiny $\chi^2_{\mathrm{token}}$ (per-step healthy), KL $\sim\!10^{-4}$ (near-on-policy), PPL ratio essentially 1. The two clamp settings ($c=2$ and $c=4$) are \emph{statistically identical} on the three pointwise metrics --- the only column they differ on is $\chi^2_{\mathrm{seq}}$, which jumps from $5\!\times\!10^{3}$ (clamp 2) to $6\!\times\!10^{6}$ (clamp 4).
\item \textbf{This three-orders-of-magnitude gap on $\chi^2_{\mathrm{seq}}$ is a feature, not a bug.} What it says is: with a looser outer clamp, the prefix product is allowed to accumulate more sequence-level drift, which $\chi^2_{\mathrm{seq}}$ faithfully reports; but because PPO clip continues to gate every $r_t$ at the per-token level, the per-step drift ($\chi^2_{\mathrm{token}}$, KL) is unaffected, and the gradient remains usable. The outer clamp acts on a quantity ($\sum_t\log r_t$) that is downstream of, and dominated by, the per-token gate. It is precisely the redundancy described in §3.4 made empirically visible.
\item \texttt{SC-min-prefix} / \texttt{SC-identity} sit in the "collapsed-but-looks-fine-at-sequence-level" regime. The 0.6--1.3 nat KL signals real off-policyness; their \emph{low} $\chi^2_{\mathrm{seq}}$ is a red herring caused by mode concentration, not by genuine distribution match.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Take-away.} (i) Looking at $\chi^2_{\mathrm{seq}}$ alone (or KL alone) is not enough to detect off-policy drift in LLM RL --- the two $\chi^2$ metrics together act as a two-pointer test: \emph{both must be small} for the rollout/training distributions to actually agree. (ii) The fact that $\chi^2_{\mathrm{seq}}$ reacts strongly to the SC-clamp radius while KL and $\chi^2_{\mathrm{token}}$ do not is direct evidence that PPO clip alone --- not the outer SC clamp --- is what gates the meaningful per-step drift in our setting. The SC clamp's job is to keep the (already-tame) sequence-level accumulation bounded for downstream variance, not to enforce on-policyness.

\subsection*{5.5 In one sentence}

Putting the exact prefix product $\rho_{1:t-1}$ back into the per-token loss --- with vllm's rollout log-probs as $\pi_\beta$ and a hard mask (not a numerical clamp) on tokens whose $w$ or $r$ falls outside the trust band --- raises training reward from $0.68$ (GRPO) to $0.75$+, prevents the GRPO val-acc collapse, halves wall-clock per step, and introduces no new tunable hyperparameter beyond the standard PPO clip $\epsilon$.

\section*{6 $\cdot$ Discussion}

\subsection*{6.1 Why \texttt{SC-mask} is the simplest thing that works}

The case for \texttt{SC-mask} is structural, not clever:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item It is the unique unbiased state-correction in the family of per-token IS estimators (Theorem~\ref{thm:is-unbiased}).
\item Its only ingredient is the prefix product $\rho_{i,1:t-1}=\prod_{j<t} r_{i,j}$, a function of quantities every PPO/GRPO trainer already computes.
\item The naïve numerical clamp on $w\!\cdot\! r$ is unstable in LLM RL because of pathological-token gradient corruption; treating the clamp as a hard \emph{mask} on the per-token gradient (rather than a magnitude truncation) eliminates this failure mode (§3.4).
\item No baseline network, no extra forward pass, no extra hyperparameter beyond the standard PPO clip $\epsilon$.
\end{itemize}

The first three points are not new theorems; they are observations about LLMs' deterministic-MDP structure and tokenisation noise that the GRPO-family literature has been working around rather than using. \texttt{SC-mask} simply puts the missing factor back, with the gating discipline that LLM rollouts demand.

\subsection*{6.2 \texttt{SC(identity)} $\neq$ "GRPO minus state correction"}

The only methodological difference between the GRPO baseline and SC(identity) is the source of \texttt{old\_log\_prob}:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{GRPO baseline} recomputes \texttt{old\_log\_prob} via a fresh actor forward pass at training precision. At step 1, $\pi_\theta=\pi_{\text{old}}$ exactly, so $r_t\equiv 1$ for every token and no token is clipped.
\item \textbf{SC(identity)} reads \texttt{old\_log\_prob} directly from the vllm rollout engine (bypass mode). Vllm runs in lower precision with a different attention kernel, so its log-probs disagree with the actor's recomputed log-probs by a small but non-zero amount --- the \textbf{training-inference mismatch}. Consequently $r_t\ne 1$ on the very first step, and a measurable fraction of tokens fall outside $[1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]$.
\end{itemize}

This is why SC(identity) logs \texttt{actor/ppo\_clip\_frac} averaging 26 \% while the GRPO baseline stays under 2 \% --- \textbf{not because $w\equiv 1$ is unstable}, but because the vllm-vs-actor reference disagrees slightly and the clip gate catches that disagreement. The loss form itself is the same: SC(identity) and GRPO produce numerically identical gradients on in-band tokens (via $\nabla r_t = r_t\nabla\log\pi_\theta$).

Two implications:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{The clean comparison is among the SC runs}, which all share the bypass-mode \texttt{old\_log\_prob}, so \texttt{ppo\_clip\_frac} is governed only by how each $w_{i,t}$ choice interacts with the same reference. On that apples-to-apples comparison, \texttt{SC-mask} has the highest training reward, the lowest total exclusion, and the most stable \texttt{joint\_active\_frac} --- attributable purely to $w_{i,t}$.
\item \textbf{The GRPO-vs-SC macro comparison} (val-acc, response length, total wall-clock) is still meaningful, but the precise clip-fraction gap between GRPO baseline and SC(identity) should not be read as "removing the state correction causes instability"; it's the reference-precision gap.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{6.3 Exploration preservation: \texttt{pg\_loss} $\approx$ 0 and stable entropy}

\texttt{SC-mask}'s training reward climbs fastest (§6.4) while simultaneously logging near-zero \texttt{pg\_loss}. This is the data signature of a structural property: the per-token weight $w_{i,t}=\rho_{i,1:t-1}$ \emph{decouples each rollout's update from its own advantage}, so the policy does not collapse into the few rollouts that happened to score high.

\textbf{Step 1 $\cdot$ What small \texttt{pg\_loss} means structurally.} At fixed $t$, the SC loss (dropping constants) is

\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E[\text{pg\_loss}] \;=\; -\mathbb E_i\!\left[w_{i,t}\,r_{i,t}\,\hat A_{i,t}\,\log\pi_\theta(o_{i,t})\right].
\end{equation*}

GRPO-advantage normalisation forces $\sum_i\hat A_{i,t}=0$ within each group, so the constant-offset part cancels automatically and only a covariance survives:

\begin{equation*}
\mathbb E[\text{pg\_loss}] \;\propto\; \operatorname{Cov}_i\!\Big(\,w_{i,t}\,r_{i,t}\,\log\pi_\theta(o_{i,t}),\ \hat A_{i,t}\,\Big).
\end{equation*}

So $|\text{pg\_loss}|\approx 0$ says exactly one thing: \textbf{within each group, the quantity $w\!\cdot\! r\!\cdot\!\log\pi_\theta$ is near-independent of the rollout's advantage}. Good rollouts (large $\hat A$) and bad rollouts (small $\hat A$) become statistically indistinguishable along that axis --- which is exactly what one wants from a state-correction term: $w_{i,t}$ should reflect \emph{drift of the prefix from $\pi_\beta$}, not \emph{the rollout's reward}.

The gradient itself is still healthy --- \texttt{grad\_norm} is $O(10^{-1}\sim 10^0)$ across all SC runs. Small \texttt{pg\_loss} is not weak training; it is the prefix-product weight passively decoupling the logged scalar from the group-constant advantage offset.

\item \textbf{SC-identity and SC-min-prefix}: large \texttt{pg\_loss} (0.19--0.22) is direct structural evidence that $w$ is correlated with $\hat A$ --- \texttt{SC-identity} via the bypass-mode perturbation, \texttt{SC-min-prefix} because small $\min r$ tracks bad rollouts. The correlation shows up macroscopically as large \texttt{ppo\_clip\_frac} (13 \% and 26 \%) and, for \texttt{SC-identity}, response-length inflation.
\item \textbf{SC-mask}: the raw prefix product has no structural $\hat A$-coupling, so the covariance vanishes by construction. Both clamp settings give \textbf{stable response length (\textasciitilde{}540), stable \texttt{ppo\_clip\_frac} (\textasciitilde{}0.6 \%), and preserved val-acc} --- the macroscopic fingerprint of preserved exploration.

\textbf{Step 3 $\cdot$ The feedback-loop mechanism.} Why does the prefix product damp entropy in practice?

\emph{(a) GRPO: uncontained positive feedback on uncertainty.} Under GRPO ($w=1$), the per-token gradient contribution is $r_{i,t}\hat A_{i,t}\nabla\log\pi_\theta$. Consider a "slightly uncertain" token that happens to sit in a high-advantage rollout. After the update its probability rises, so at the next iteration $r_{i,t}>1$ for this token. The gradient weight is now larger than last step; the update pushes the probability up \emph{more aggressively}; the next $r_{i,t}$ grows further; and so on. This is a classical positive-feedback loop: \textbf{uncertainty is rewarded with amplification}, because nothing in the loss references \emph{how much the upstream prefix has already drifted}. The result is entropy runaway.

\emph{(b) \texttt{SC-mask}: the prefix product damps the loop, the mask kills pathological tokens.} With $w_{i,t} = \rho_{i,1:t-1} = \prod_{j<t} r_{i,j}$, when a token's prefix drifts upward, its downstream $\rho_{i,1:t-1}$ also grows, and the mask then rejects the token entirely once $w$ leaves the trust band; conversely when a prefix drifts downward, $\rho_{i,1:t-1}$ shrinks and the gradient weight shrinks with it. The amplification chain from (a) is broken in two complementary ways: drift in the prefix is reflected back into the gradient weight (per-token soft damping), and outliers are masked out altogether (per-token hard rejection).

\emph{(c) Why \texttt{SC-min-prefix} only partially works.} $\min_{j<t}r_{i,j}$ tracks the worst single position in the prefix, not the cumulative drift. A rollout with one short bad prefix step but otherwise on-policy gets penalised globally; a rollout with many small drifts but no single bad step does not. So the damping signal is unfaithful, and the gate ends up firing on PPO clip at 13\% rather than on the prefix product itself.

The empirical signature \textbf{$|\text{pg\_loss}|\approx 0$ + stable \texttt{grad\_norm} + stable \texttt{ppo\_clip\_frac} + stable response length} is the four-way coherent fingerprint of the regime in which the prefix-product weight is doing its job: closing the entropy-runaway loop without needing any additional baseline or critic.

\end{itemize}

\section*{7 $\cdot$ Takeaway}

The state ratio that every GRPO-family method drops is, in LLMs, \textbf{not intractable} --- it is an already-computed product of per-token log-ratios $\rho_{1:t-1}=\prod_{j<t} r_{i,j}$. The textbook objection (exponential variance) is also moot: PPO's standard token-level clip already gates every $r_j$ at the per-token level, and any single-position pathology (typo, hallucination, low-$\pi_\beta$ rare token) is masked out of the gradient before it can multiply downstream weights. What remains is a tame geometric envelope, well within the band a wide outer clamp catches as a redundant safety net.

This makes \texttt{SC-mask} the simplest possible answer to "how should an LLM RL trainer use the prefix product?": put $\rho_{1:t-1}$ back into the per-token loss, and gate every token whose ($w\!\cdot\! r$) leaves the trust band as a hard mask on its gradient. By Theorem~\ref{thm:is-unbiased} the resulting estimator is exact (unbiased) under $\pi_\beta$ on the kept tokens; the mask handles per-token outliers (typos, low-mass rare tokens), and there is \emph{no new tunable hyperparameter} beyond the PPO clip $\epsilon$ that GRPO already exposes.

On Qwen2.5-1.5B / MATH / 105 steps, against the stock GRPO baseline:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{+8 pp training reward} (0.756 vs 0.677)
\item \textbf{+9 pp val-acc at step 105} (0.551 vs 0.465) --- GRPO overfits, \texttt{SC-mask} doesn't
\item \textbf{$-$48 \% wall-clock per step} ($c{=}4$: 301 s vs.\ 580 s); matches GSPO's throughput
\item \textbf{Robust to clamp radius}: $c\!\in\!\{2,4\}$ produce statistically identical training dynamics, and the SC clamp activation rate moves by 6$\times$ between them while PPO clip activation, KL, and $\chi^2_{\mathrm{token}}$ are unchanged --- direct evidence that PPO clip alone, not the outer clamp, gates the meaningful drift
\end{itemize}

No extra parameters, no extra forward passes, no extra reward shaping, no extra hyperparameter --- the correction the literature discarded is already there waiting to be added back, and the standard PPO clip already takes care of why people thought it could not be.
